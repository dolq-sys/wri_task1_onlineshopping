Model answer · 201 words

The bar chart compares the shares of Australian internet users in six age groups who made purchases online in 2007 and 2009.

Overall, online shopping participation increased across all six age groups. Besides, the most substantial rise was among people aged 36–45, who overtook those from 26 to 35 to become the most active online shoppers by 2009. Internet users aged 65 and over were the least likely to buy goods online in both years.

With regard to the three youngest age groups, around 31% of internet users aged 26–35 used the internet to shop in 2007, compared with 26% of those aged 36–45. Over the next two years, the former figure edged up to 33%, while the latter surged by 12 percentage points to 38%, the highest figure on the chart. Among 15–25-year-olds, the share who shopped online rose more modestly, from 20% to 23%.

Looking at the remaining age cohorts, the proportion of internet users who made online purchases rose from 22% to 27% among 46–55-year-olds and from 12% to 18% among those aged 56–65. The figure for the oldest group more than doubled, climbing from roughly 5% to 12%, the same level recorded by 56–65-year-olds two years earlier.

A3 · Numbers: from … to, by, percentage points

rose from 26% to 38%  =  rose by 12 percentage points
to = con số đích đến  ·  by = mức chênh lệch  ·  38% − 26% = 12 percentage points (điểm phần trăm), không phải "12%".
Số đọc ước lượng từ biểu đồ → thêm around / roughly / approximately.
  1. Among 46–55-year-olds, the figure rose 22% 27%.
  2. The share of 36–45-year-olds surged 12 percentage points.
  3. The figure for 36–45-year-olds surged by 12 to 38%.
  4. The oldest group's figure climbed from 5% to 12%. (khoảng)
  5. Over the next two years, the 26–35 figure edged up 33%.
